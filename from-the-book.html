Case Study 02 — Editorial Design

From the Book
to the Streets

A redesign of the cover and interior of an essay collection on lettering and type, from choosing a typeface and grid through to a hand-finished, bound copy.

Module
TG2ED, Editorial Design
Role
Cover, interior layout, typesetting & binding
Tools
InDesign
Timeline
Year 2, 2025
01

The Brief

Redesign From the Book to the Streets, a collection of essays on typography in public spaces, published by the Hochschule für Grafik und Buchkunst Leipzig. The project covered a typographic cover, the interior layout of a chapter, the prelims and endmatter, and finally printing and binding a finished copy.

02

Type & Grid

I set the same text in four serif typefaces, Inria Serif, Haboro Serif, Noto Serif and Capitolium, to compare them on the printed page rather than on screen. Capitolium won. Its large x-height and condensed width keep long passages readable, and it has the small caps, ligatures and old-style numerals a scholarly text needs.

The grid is 6 × 3, with a wide outer margin for captions and footnotes at the foot of the page. At first I set the footnotes in a different typeface, but feedback showed they looked disconnected from the text, so I changed them to Capitolium at a smaller size. Contrast should support the hierarchy, not break it.

03

The Cover

I studied typographic covers, from post-war poetry covers inspired by Berthold Wolpe to the work of David Pearson, and designed nine versions with the title as the only image. I started with the classic red and white, then moved to black with red, orange and yellow. These combinations felt more distinctive and suggest road signs, which ties in with the book's subject of lettering on the street.

The final cover stacks the title in large yellow type on dark grey, with a bright yellow inside cover so the colour carries through when you open the book.

“Match the intensity of the title with the sizing, tone and temper of the book.”

— David Pearson, guest lecture
04

The Interior

Body text runs across two columns, with captions in the margin and footnotes below. The hardest part was keeping every image and footnote on the same spread as its reference, while still giving the archive images enough room. I learned that the designer is responsible for treating images with respect: sizing them properly and never cropping them.

The contents page uses tabs and leaders for clean alignment, and the bibliography picks out authors' names in bold.

05

Print & Finish

To make the yellow title shine like spot UV, I tested three clear finishes by hand: PVA glue, gloss decoupage medium and Mod Podge. I chose PVA because it left no brush marks. Then I printed, trimmed and bound the book myself.

06

Reflection

Looking back, the PVA wasn't shiny enough. Mod Podge, or clear nail polish as some classmates used, would have given a stronger spot UV effect. I'd also take more care with the making: more precise trimming so the cover lines up with the text block, and laminating the cover so the black doesn't rub or crack at the folds.

This project, together with a studio visit to DK, made me seriously consider a career in editorial design. I'm already using these skills on the student union magazine.

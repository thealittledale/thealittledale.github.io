A mobile app for finding and booking local community spaces, designed so that people with low digital confidence can book a venue without needing to ask anyone for help.

Module
TG2UX, User-Centred Design
Role
Research, wireframes & UI design (group project)
Tools
Figma, Stark
Timeline
Year 2, 2025–26
01

The Brief

A local council has found that many of its services are underused because the apps and websites behind them are out of date. Our brief was to pick one service area, research who uses it, and design an app prototype in Figma that meets their needs.

We chose booking community spaces: village halls, community centres, sports facilities and libraries. They're used for everything from fitness classes to support groups, but the ways to book them are often confusing. As a result, a lot of community buildings sit empty.

02

Research & Insight

We looked at the main existing booking platforms, AllBooked and PeerSpace. Both had clear layouts and live support, but key details such as location, price and restrictions were hard to find, and their interfaces could shut out less confident users.

Older adults turned out to be some of the heaviest users of community buildings, especially village halls, libraries and community cafés. Yet one in seven people aged 65 and over can't turn on a device and log in to an account. Younger people book these spaces the least. That gave us two very different users to design for.

03

Personas & Needs

From the research we created two personas: an older user with low digital confidence, who represents most people using these spaces, and a younger, tech-savvy user, to show how the app could bring new people in. At the IBM workshop we wrote several needs statements for each, then cut them down to the one thing each person most needs from a booking.

Hunainza 65

  • Retired
  • Just moved to a rural village
  • New to booking venues

Feels disconnected from his new village and wants to host a poker night to meet people. The event has to be strictly 18+.

Needs statementNeeds a way to arrange a poker night while keeping it strictly 18+, so that he can keep the event age-appropriate and meet new people his age.

Avery 25

  • Games developer
  • Lives in Bristol
  • Autistic

Wants to book a venue for a friend's birthday party with catering. Can become overwhelmed easily and would rather not phone or email strangers to get answers.

Needs statementNeeds a way to know the policies on bringing food and drinks to a venue, so that she can book a birthday party without contacting other people for information.

04

Process

We studied how successful apps handle search, filters and booking. From Airbnb we took the idea of a map view, which a tutor also suggested, so users can see at a glance how far away a venue is instead of reading it in small print. From Vinted we took quick filter toggles at the top of the results, with a separate panel for more detailed filtering.

We then sketched in rapid rounds: four frames each, discuss what works, sketch again. The strongest ideas became a low-fidelity flow in Figma, with as few steps and pages as possible.

06

Inclusive Design

Colour. Blue is the main colour because it's linked with calm and reliability, which helps when you're making decisions, and it stays readable for people with colour blindness. Neutral greys keep the background quiet, and green marks successful actions.

Testing. We checked the design in Stark under all four main types of colour blindness, as well as for contrast and type size.

Type. IBM Plex Serif for titles and headings, DM Sans for body text and labels, because its tall x-height stays legible at small sizes. We kept to two typefaces to avoid visual overload.

07

Outcome & Reflection

The project produced a research report, a pitch to the council, a clickable Figma prototype with a recorded walkthrough, and my own illustrated report on the design process.

The IBM workshop was the turning point. Mapping everyone affected by a booking showed problems we wouldn't otherwise have spotted. If I took the app further, I'd make the secondary buttons bigger, as I did with the main confirm buttons, to better support users with mobility issues.
